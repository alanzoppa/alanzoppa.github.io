Principal Component Analysis finds the 3 orthogonal axes that capture the most variance. It is a linear projection — no warping, no folding, no inventing structure. Distances between points are globally faithful to the original 4096-dim cosine geometry. With only 3 components it retains 67% of total variance. The translucent spheres show each word's residual — the L2 distance between its true position and its 3D reconstruction. Bigger sphere = more discarded information. The spheres are round because PCA residuals are orthogonal to the displayed space — the uncertainty is all "depth" into invisible dimensions, not directional bias in the ones you can see.

t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding optimizes for local neighborhood preservation. Points close in 4096-dim space will be close here — but distances between clusters are meaningless. Two clusters appearing near each other does NOT mean they're semantically related. The algorithm warps global geometry to make local structure legible. KL divergence (0.3257) measures how well 3D neighbor probabilities match the originals. Kruskal stress (0.30) is higher than PCA because global distances are sacrificed. Best used for: spotting unexpected local clusters, not comparing group distances.

Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection attempts to preserve both local neighborhoods and some global structure — a middle ground between PCA and t-SNE. n_neighbors=5 controls how many local points define the manifold (low = very local); min_dist=0.1 controls how tightly points can pack. With only 16 data points, UMAP has very few neighbors to build a reliable manifold from, which explains the high stress (0.49). The clusters are real, but their spacing and orientation are algorithmic artifacts. Inter-cluster distances are suggestive but not trustworthy.
